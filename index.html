    How the metro makes music

    1. The timetable

      BMRCL publishes a timetable for each line. A community dataset turns those into a standard format listing every trip of the day, about 900 on a weekday.

    2. The trains

      Your browser works out where each of today's trains should be at this moment in Bengaluru, and moves it along the real track. It follows the timetable, not live GPS, so real trains can be a few minutes off.

    3. The notes

      When a train reaches a station, that station's note plays on its line's instrument. Notes rise the further a station is from Majestic (RV Road on the Yellow Line), so trains heading into the city fall towards Sa, the home note.

    The orchestra

    LineInstrumentHome note atStations

    Underneath, a tanpura holds Sa and Pa (or Ma, for ragas without Pa). Majestic and RV Road, where lines meet, add a soft gong.

    The ragas

    All three lines play in the same raga, so their notes always fit together. Each one uses five notes, which keeps chance meetings between trains consonant. Auto moves through Mohanam in the morning, Madhyamavati at midday, Hamsadhwani for the evening rush and Shivaranjani at night.

    Notes snap to a gentle 80 bpm pulse. At 20× speed some arrivals share a beat or wait for the next one.
